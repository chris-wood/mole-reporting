\noindent
Let $j$ be a private Anchor tag, and let $H,R$ be fixed, independent
generators of a group of prime order $q$. A blinded commitment to the tag is
\[
  C=jH+\rho R,
\]
where $\rho$ is fresh randomness. The blinding is important: if the
commitment were only $jH$, anyone could try every Anchor tag and identify
$j$.

\paragraph{Sharing and recombining the commitment.}
Share both scalars modulo $q$ between two aggregators:
\[
  j=j_0+j_1\pmod q,\qquad
  \rho=\rho_0+\rho_1\pmod q.
\]
Each aggregator $m\in\{0,1\}$ computes a group point from its own private
shares, $C_m=j_mH+\rho_mR$. The points add to the original commitment:
\[
  C_0+C_1
   =(j_0+j_1)H+(\rho_0+\rho_1)R
   =jH+\rho R=C.
\]
The aggregators can exchange the points and compare their sum with a public
$C$ without revealing their scalar shares or reconstructing $j$.

\paragraph{The VDAF field mismatch.}
In the proposed Field128 DAP histogram, the VDAF's private tag shares $t_0,t_1$
instead live in a field of order $p$, while the commitment group has order
$q>2p$. For the private tag $t=(t_0+t_1)\bmod p$, the canonical integer
shares satisfy
\[
  \operatorname{int}(t_0)+\operatorname{int}(t_1)
   \in\{\operatorname{int}(t),\operatorname{int}(t)+p\}.
\]
Thus, using the VDAF's actual tag shares and group blinding shares to form
$P_m=\operatorname{int}(t_m)H+\rho_mR$ yields either $P_0+P_1=C$ or
$P_0+P_1=C+pH$ when $C$ commits to the same tag. The second case is the
field carry, not a second allowed Anchor. Sharing the tag modulo $q$ would
avoid this carry, but would require a separate proof that those shares match
the VDAF histogram bucket.

\paragraph{What equality establishes.}
Point addition binds the combined shares to the value inside $C$; it does
not, on its own, establish that this value is the credential's Anchor.
The MOLE presentation must prove that $C$ contains the credential's signed
hidden $j$, and the VDAF must verify that its private tag identifies the
selected one-hot bucket. Both aggregators must enforce the commitment check
before counting the report.
