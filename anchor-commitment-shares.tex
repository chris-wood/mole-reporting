\noindent
Let $j$ be a private Anchor tag, and let $H,R$ be fixed, independent
generators of a group of prime order $q$. A blinded commitment to the tag is
\[
  C=jH+\rho R,
\]
where $\rho$ is fresh randomness. The blinding is important: if the
commitment were only $jH$, anyone could try every Anchor tag and identify
$j$.

\paragraph{Projecting the index.}
In ordinary Field128 Prio3Histogram, aggregator $m\in\{0,1\}$ receives its
private share $v_{m,i}$ of the histogram for each bucket $i$. It derives a
1-based index share from those same input shares over the field of order $p$:
\[
  t_m=\sum_i(i+1)v_{m,i}\pmod p.
\]
The normal Prio3 proof establishes that the combined measurement is one-hot,
so $t=(t_0+t_1)\bmod p=j$. No separately supplied index share or new VDAF
measurement coordinate is needed. Each aggregator also receives only its own
private scalar $\rho_m$, with $\rho_0+\rho_1=\rho\pmod q$.

\paragraph{Checking the commitment.}
Each aggregator computes $P_m=\operatorname{int}(t_m)H+\rho_mR$ and exchanges
this blinded point with the other aggregator. The commitment group has order
$q>2p$. The canonical integer representatives of the Field128 shares satisfy
\[
  \operatorname{int}(t_0)+\operatorname{int}(t_1)
   \in\{\operatorname{int}(t),\operatorname{int}(t)+p\}.
\]
Thus, when the report counts the same Anchor as the commitment,
$P_0+P_1\in\{C,C+pH\}$. The second case is the field carry, not a second
allowed Anchor. The aggregators compare the points without revealing their
scalar shares or reconstructing $j$.

\paragraph{What equality establishes.}
Point addition binds the combined shares to the value inside $C$; it does
not, on its own, establish that this value is the credential's Anchor.
The MOLE presentation must prove that $C$ contains the credential's signed
hidden $j$, and ordinary Prio3Histogram must verify the one-hot bucket from
whose shares the index is projected. Both aggregators must enforce the
commitment check before counting the report. Neither receives the full
opening $\rho$: that would expose the small-domain index by enumeration.
